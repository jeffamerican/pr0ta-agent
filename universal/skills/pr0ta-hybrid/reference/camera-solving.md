# Recovering a camera from source footage

Use this for hybrid-stage footage whose camera movement should drive a virtual-set camera. The internal tool uses Blender's native background feature tracker and camera solver. It spends no generation credits. It does not recover absolute metric stage positions from footage alone.

1. Import/probe the **original** video. Choose one continuous, constant-frame-rate shot. Keep original metadata intact. Normalize rotation and pixel aspect and reduce to at most 1920 pixels on the longest side if needed.
2. Submit `camera_solve_submit` with `request` containing `source_asset_id`, a unique `idempotency_key` (8–128 letters/digits/underscore/hyphen), and `settings`. The source is at most 512 MiB and 9000 frames; the selected shot is 16–900 frames, up to 30 seconds at 1–120 fps. `start_frame` is zero based and `end_frame` exclusive.
3. Leave `focal_length_pixels` unset for unknown-lens mode. The worker inspects original-file lens, sensor and camera tags before making a silent tracked plate. Convertible metadata supplies a starting prior; otherwise several focal hypotheses are tried. Focal length alone and lens names do not establish pixel focal length without the sensor/crop. The receipt names the origin and warns about unverified video crop or zoom. An explicit pixel focal overrides metadata. `refine_focal_length` defaults true; `refine_distortion` defaults false.
4. Supply `exclude_regions` as normalized top-left `{left, top, right, bottom}` rectangles covering moving performers, vehicles or reflections throughout the shot. These are static exclusion zones, not tracked actor masks. Keep enough stationary stage texture visible. `motion_model=general` assumes parallax; `tripod` is rotation-only and cannot measure camera translation or scene depth.
5. Poll `tasks_get`. Success supplies `solve_asset_id`, `blend_asset_id`, and `tracked_plate_asset_id`. Download the latter two as `camera_solve.blend` and `tracked_shot.mp4` into one folder, relinking the movie if needed. Inspect native tracks in Blender. Never claim a failed or incomplete solve succeeded.
6. Read `camera_solve_get(solve_asset_id=...)`. Inspect error, per-frame support, calibration candidates, source-frame timing, sampled poses and sparse points. A low error is insufficient: moving actors and an incorrect lens can fit. `calibration_status=ambiguous` means similarly good fits disagree about the lens; `weakly_constrained` flags too little camera rotation to trust self-calibration, even if every solver seed converges. Verify against the stage/set geometry and overlays at beginning, middle and end.
7. Use `camera_solve_place` only with an explicit proposed alignment: `request={solve_asset_id, environment_id, scale, rotation_degrees:[x,y,z], translation:[x,y,z], world_units:"scene_units"}`. Rotation is XYZ Euler degrees in the set's Blender Z-up frame. The same similarity transforms all cameras and points and saves a **new** JSON asset. Scale, origin and floor require real correspondences or stage information; this tool does not match sparse points to the set automatically. The new result remains `placed_for_review` and `metric_scale_verified=false`.
8. After checking that placement, `camera_solve_get(..., include_track=true)` returns `blender_track` for `blender_job_submit`'s `scene_plan.shot_cameras[].track` or `coverage_from_shotlist.tracks`. It uses seconds, Blender Z-up locations, XYZ Euler degrees and normalized 36 mm horizontal-gate lenses. Do not feed Blender coordinates directly to a glTF/Three viewer without conversion.

Render-track export rejects distortion, noncentered principal points, nonsquare pixels and out-of-contract lenses. The editable tracking clip retains distortion coefficients, but a plain render camera does not apply distortion automatically. Undistort the plate and/or redistort the render with a verified compositing workflow before calling it matched.

The default maximum average reprojection error is 2 px (configurable up to 3). A solve must cover every frame with at least eight observations and twelve reliable reconstructed tracks. These are rejection thresholds, not approval. Constant lens is assumed; rolling shutter, zoom, blur, cuts and weak parallax can invalidate reconstruction.

Retry dispatch with the same key and identical inputs. Use a new key for changed settings or a new solve attempt. Cancellation removes queued worker work; a running native process may still finish, but cannot revive a canceled task.
